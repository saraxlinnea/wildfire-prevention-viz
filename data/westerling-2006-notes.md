# Westerling 2006 — snowmelt timing and western forest wildfire

Literature chart on the Patterns tab. **Not** a repository-derived snowmelt time series.

## Paper

Westerling, A. L., Hidalgo, H. G., Cayan, D. R., & Swetnam, T. W. (2006).
Warming and Earlier Spring Increase Western U.S. Forest Wildfire Activity.
*Science*, 313(5789), 940–943. [doi:10.1126/science.1128834](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128834)

## Population

Large **western U.S. forest** wildfires (1970–2003 analysis), not all-land NIFC national acres and not NICC GACC totals on this page.

## Charted statistics (`westerling-snowmelt-tercile.csv`)

Among large western forest wildfires in the snowmelt analysis window:

| Snowmelt timing | Share of fires | Share of area burned |
|---|---:|---:|
| Early tercile | 56% | 72% |
| Late tercile | 11% | 4% |
| Middle tercile | 33% | 24% |

Early and late shares are reported in the paper. Middle shares are the remainder (100 − early − late) and are labeled as derived on the page.

## Other prose statistics (claims; not on the bar chart)

- Average fire-season length increased by **78 days (64%)**, comparing 1970–1986 with 1987–2003.
- Average discovery-to-control duration rose from **7.5 to 37.1 days**.
- Fire-season length vs regional spring/summer temperature: Spearman **ρ ≈ 0.61**.

## Why not a modern SNOTEL chart yet

A current annual snowmelt / melt-out series needs station selection, quality control, and geography aligned to western forest fire area. That is a separate Phase 2 data task. This page ships the paper’s snowmelt-tercile result as attributed literature.

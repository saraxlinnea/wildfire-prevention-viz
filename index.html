An Ounce of Prevention

National wildfire activity, 1983-2026

As of June 18, 2026, more than 2.6 million acres have burned nationally, about 63% above the 10-year average to date. Peak fire season starts in summer. In 2025 the Forest Service treated 35% fewer acres for wildfire risk than the year before.

Four tabs: national fire outcomes, drivers and federal context, exploratory coupling, and how to read the series. This page does not claim one line caused another.

AccuWeather forecast range appears in million acres view only. In most years, most U.S. acres burned occur in western states; this line is national.

Western fire-season dryness and federal fuels treatment. These series use different geographies, seasons, and calendars than national burn totals.

VPD (vapor pressure deficit) measures how strongly the atmosphere pulls moisture from fuels: a physical dryness index from gridMET, May through September, west of 100°W.

DSCI (Drought Severity Coverage Index) summarizes modeled drought stress from U.S. Drought Monitor categories. It can stay elevated after rain if deep soil moisture is still depleted.

Treatment acres count federal hazardous-fuels work reported by agency. Acres treated are not the same as risk reduced; effects can lag and depend on where treatment occurs.

How to read this
Outcomes are national acres burned (NIFC, 1983 onward). YTD and forecast figures are partial or speculative. The rolling band uses prior full calendar years; header YTD uses NIFC's same-date 10-year average.

Drivers & context cover western dryness and federal treatment on different geographies and calendars. VPD is physical; DSCI is modeled drought stress; treatment acres are reported work, not proven risk reduction.

Coupling shows statistical overlap only (16 years, 2010-2025). Western VPD tracks national acres more closely than national drought index in this window (r ≈ 0.63 vs 0.10). That reflects geography and measurement, not a single cause.

What this is not. A model of fire causes. The page does not claim that 2025 prevention cuts caused the 2026 fire season.
Scientific limits
Geography. National acres include all states; VPD and western DSCI cover western fire-prone areas. Comparing them is common in practice but imperfect.

Calendar. Burn acres and DSCI are calendar years; Interior treatment is fiscal year (Oct 1). Fire season spans months, but these series are annual.

Correlation. Pearson r describes linear co-movement in a short sample. It does not control for climate oscillations, vegetation, ignitions, suppression, or development in the wildland-urban interface.

Policy. Acres treated measure effort, not outcomes. Hazard reduction depends on treatment type, location, and whether subsequent weather overwhelms fuels work.

Forecasts. The AccuWeather 2026 range is a conditional projection, not observed data.
Policy context
In 2025 the Forest Service treated 2.6 million acres for wildfire risk, down from 4.1 million in 2024. In March 2026 USDA announced plans to close 57 of 77 Forest Service research stations; many study fire behavior and smoke forecasting. Same date in 2022 saw about 3.1 million acres burned YTD, the highest in NIFC's table; 2026 ranks second.
On the data and sources
  • Acres burned: total U.S. figures, all land types, calendar year, NIFC annual statistics. Standardized reporting from 1983; pre-1983 figures are not comparable.
  • 2026 point: partial year through June 18 only (2,627,549 acres). Not a full-year figure.
  • 10-year baseline band: min-to-max of the ten prior calendar years of NIFC acres burned. Computed from chart data, not the CRS flat 7.2M reference.
  • Forest Service treatment: NPR analysis of USFS FACTS database, cross-checked by Center for Western Priorities. Consistent figures from 2023 onward only.
  • Interior Department treatment: BLM, NPS, BIA, and FWS lands, fiscal year starting October 1, doi.gov, 2018-2024.
  • DSCI: annual average of weekly readings, U.S. Drought Monitor API. National (conus) and NWS Western Region (aoi=WR). Available from 2000. 2026 values are 24-week averages through June 16.
  • VPD: western U.S. fire-season mean from gridMET via OPeNDAP (Abatzoglou 2013). May through September, west of 100°W, 1979-2025.
  • 2026 forecast: AccuWeather published 2026 wildfire season projection, 5.5 to 8 million acres.
  • Research stations: USDA Forest Service reorganization announced March 31, 2026.
  • Correlations: exploratory views on the Coupling tab; matrix in correlation-matrix.csv and notebook. Not causal evidence.
  • Data processing: drought from U.S. Drought Monitor API; western fire-season VPD from gridMET (Abatzoglou 2013). CSVs and notebooks in the GitHub repository.

This page is not claiming that cutting prevention in 2025 caused the 2026 fire season. It shows two things happening at the same time and leaves the conclusion to the reader.

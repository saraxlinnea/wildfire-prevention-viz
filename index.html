An Ounce of Prevention

Our forests on fire

In 2025, the Forest Service treated 35% fewer acres for wildfire risk than in 2024. The agency is run by a former lumber industry executive whose boss spent years fighting the Forest Service in court before the Senate confirmed him to oversee it. As of May 29, 2026, more than 2.3 million acres have already burned. Peak fire season starts in summer. AccuWeather forecasts 5.5 to 8 million acres burned by year end.

Benjamin Franklin wrote "an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure" in 1735 in a letter begging Philadelphia to take fire safety seriously. The federal government is going into the worst start to a fire season in recent memory having done half the prevention work it did the year before.

The ounce was always going to be cheaper.

On the data. Acres burned are total national figures across all land types, sourced from NIFC annual statistics 2015-2025. The 2026 bar reflects acres burned through May 29 and is a partial year not comparable to full-year figures. Forest Service hazardous fuels treatment data is available consistently from 2023 onward via NPR's analysis of agency records and the Center for Western Priorities. Interior Department treatment data covers BLM, NPS, BIA, and FWS lands from 2018-2024, sourced from doi.gov. Prevention and fire outcomes do not correlate perfectly year to year — drought, ignition patterns, and weather conditions drive annual variation. The relationship between prevention investment and fire outcomes is long-term, not annual. The forecast of 5.5-8 million acres is AccuWeather's published 2026 seasonal projection. This chart shows trends in the same period and does not assert direct causation between prevention acres in a given year and fire acres in the same year.

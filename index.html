An Ounce of Prevention

Our forests on fire

In 2025 the Forest Service treated 35% fewer acres for wildfire risk than the year before. As of June 18, 2026, more than 2.6 million acres have already burned nationally, about 63% above the 10-year average to date. Peak fire season starts in summer.

Five tabs: what is burning, what the atmosphere looks like, what federal agencies are treating, how the series relate (coming soon), and how to read it all. National acres burned start in 1983. I am not claiming one line caused another.

June 2026 snapshot. As of June 18, more than 2.6 million acres have burned nationally, per NIFC. That is about 63% above the 10-year average to date. Same date in 2022 saw about 3.1 million acres, the highest in NIFC's year-to-date table. 2026 ranks second, not a record, but well above normal. Peak season is still ahead. In 2025 the Forest Service treated 2.6 million acres for wildfire risk, down from 4.1 million the year before. AccuWeather projects 5.5 to 8 million acres burned by year end if conditions hold. In March 2026 USDA announced plans to close 57 of 77 Forest Service research stations. Many of those sites study fire behavior and smoke forecasting.
Known drivers
Western fire-season VPD and drought index (DSCI) track atmospheric dryness on different geographies. Federal fuels treatment covers Forest Service and Interior Department lands on fiscal and calendar years that do not line up with burn acres. None of these series share the same spatial domain or reporting calendar.
Uncertainty boundaries
2026 burn acres and DSCI readings are partial-year snapshots, not full calendar years. Interior treatment is fiscal year (Oct 1). Burn acres and DSCI are calendar year. Forest Service treatment figures are comparable from 2023 onward only. Correlation is not causation. The page shows overlap in time; it does not identify a single cause.
How to read this

Benjamin Franklin wrote "an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure" in 1735 in a letter urging Philadelphia toward fire prevention. The federal government is going into peak fire season with 2026 acres burned to date well above the 10-year average, having treated 35% fewer acres for wildfire risk than the year before.

The ounce was always going to be cheaper.

On the data and sources
  • Acres burned: total U.S. figures, all land types, calendar year, NIFC annual statistics. Standardized reporting from 1983; pre-1983 figures are not comparable.
  • 2026 point: partial year through June 18 only (2,627,549 acres). Not a full-year figure.
  • 10-year baseline band: min-to-max of the ten prior calendar years of NIFC acres burned. Computed from chart data, not the CRS flat 7.2M reference.
  • Forest Service treatment: NPR analysis of USFS FACTS database, cross-checked by Center for Western Priorities. Consistent figures from 2023 onward only.
  • Interior Department treatment: BLM, NPS, BIA, and FWS lands, fiscal year starting October 1, doi.gov, 2018-2024.
  • DSCI: annual average of weekly readings, U.S. Drought Monitor API. National (conus) and NWS Western Region (aoi=WR). Available from 2000. 2026 values are 24-week averages through June 16.
  • VPD: western U.S. fire-season mean from gridMET via OPeNDAP (Abatzoglou 2013). May through September, west of 100°W, 1979-2025.
  • 2026 forecast: AccuWeather published 2026 wildfire season projection, 5.5 to 8 million acres.
  • Research stations: USDA Forest Service reorganization announced March 31, 2026.

This page is not claiming that cutting prevention in 2025 caused the 2026 fire season. It shows two things happening at the same time and leaves the conclusion to you.

How this was built. I pulled drought figures from the U.S. Drought Monitor API and western fire-season VPD from gridMET (Abatzoglou 2013). CSVs, notebooks, and an off-page correlation study are in the GitHub repository. Correlations are exploratory and do not appear on this page.

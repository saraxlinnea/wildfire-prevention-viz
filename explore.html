Literature · western forest wildfire · not GACC/NIFC acres

Did earlier snowmelt years burn more forest?

This block is a published forest-fire history, not the all-land NIFC or GACC acres series above. Westerling et al. (Science, 2006) linked a mid-1980s jump in large western U.S. forest wildfires to warmer springs and earlier snowmelt. Average fire-season length rose by about 78 days (64%) comparing 1970-1986 with 1987-2003.

Why smoke matters
Acres burned describe where fire spread on the landscape. Wildfire smoke describes what people may breathe downwind: fine particles (PM2.5) that can worsen asthma, strain hearts and lungs, and raise premature-death risk in the epidemiology literature. A large burn year does not automatically mean your city had bad air; wind, distance, and fire type all matter.

Since at least 2016, wildfire smoke has influenced PM2.5 trends in roughly three-quarters of contiguous U.S. states, eroding about 25% of multi-decade air-quality gains in those states on average (more than half in many western states), per Burke et al. (Nature, 2023). Childs et al. (2022) estimate that the number of people exposed to at least one day with smoke PM2.5 above 100 µg/m³ per year rose sharply in the 2010s, including nearly 25 million people in 2020 alone.

Exploratory check: national acres burned and this smoke series move together where both exist (see chart note). This page does not model smoke for 2026 or map population exposure. For health guidance see CDC wildfire smoke and EPA Smoke Ready.

How to read this
Overview charts U.S. acres burned (NIFC, 1983 onward) and where those acres landed by region (NICC GACC groups: West, South, Alaska, East). Year-to-date and forecast numbers are incomplete or speculative. The shaded band on the fire chart uses prior full calendar years. Header callouts use NIFC’s same-date ten-year average, which is a different comparison.

Drivers opens with regional dryness pairings (Bars or Table), then western fire-season dryness, a dryness×acres scatter with nested co-movement numbers, and a Westerling forest-literature block (not GACC/NIFC acres).

Context covers federal treatment acres, WUI designation, and federal suppression costs (NIFC, fiscal year, nominal dollars). Treatment and suppression are mostly fiscal clocks, not the same window as calendar burn acres.

Impacts covers smoke PM2.5 (ECHO Lab v2.0 beta, 2006-2023; preliminary), national structures destroyed (NICC SIT/209, 2014-2025), and a compact federal suppression spend chart (same NIFC series as Context). Smoke × structures shared calendar window is 2014-2023 (research panel). What this page is not. A fire forecast, a full smoke or health model, or proof that a budget cut caused a fire season. It shows what moved together in public data. None of this proves that one factor caused another.
Scientific limits
Geography. National acres include every state. ERC, VPD, and western DSCI describe western fire-prone areas. Pairing western dryness with western acres is more consistent than pairing western weather with a national burn total that Alaska or the South may dominate.

Calendar. Burn acres and DSCI use calendar years. Interior treatment uses federal fiscal years starting October 1. Fire season runs from spring through fall, but these charts annualize months into one number.

Correlation. Correlation describes how two lines move together in a short sample. It does not control for El Niño, vegetation recovery, housing growth in fire-prone areas, or how hard crews fought each fire.

Policy. Treatment acres measure work reported, not lives or homes saved. Thinning and prescribed fire can lower intensity near communities when maintained, but extreme weather can overwhelm fuels work.

Forecasts. The AccuWeather 2026 range is a published projection, not observed fire data.
Policy context
In 2025 the Forest Service treated 2.6 million acres for wildfire risk, down from 4.1 million in 2024. In March 2026 USDA announced plans to close 57 of 77 Forest Service research stations. Stateline reports that affected stations include work on fire behavior and smoke forecasting. Same date in 2017 saw about 6.8 million acres burned YTD; 2018 was next (~6.5M). 2026 ranks first on NIFC’s Aug 27 same-date list at about 8.0M YTD.

Glossary (ERC, VPD, DSCI, KBDI, GACC, WUI, fiscal vs calendar year)
ERC (Energy Release Component)
Fuel-focused fire danger index from dead and live fuel moisture. Higher ERC in May-September usually means fuels can burn more intensely if ignited.
VPD (Vapor pressure deficit)
How strongly the atmosphere pulls moisture from plants and soils. High VPD dries fine fuels quickly during the western fire season.
DSCI (Drought Severity Coverage Index)
Share of land in drought categories on the U.S. Drought Monitor. Annual figures on this page are a year-to-date (or full-year) average of weekly readings, not a single map date. Useful for water supply and long-term stress; weaker match to annual acres burned than ERC or VPD here.
KBDI (Keetch-Byram Drought Index)
Southern Area drought / deep-drying index for the top soil and duff layer. 0 ≈ saturated; higher values (toward 800) mean drier. Often watched with NFDRS fuel moisture, ERC, and BI. On this page, Southeast Jan-May mean KBDI is computed from gridMET temperature and precip (not a live SACC feed).
GACC (Geographic Area Coordination Center)
Federal fire-reporting regions (West, South, Alaska, East). NICC sums GACC acres for regional share charts; western GACC totals exclude Alaska and the South.
WUI (Wildland-urban interface)
In HFR tables, a designation label for where treatment was categorized (near communities vs remote). Not the same as homes protected or risk reduced.
Federal fiscal year vs calendar year
Fiscal year runs Oct 1 through Sep 30 (e.g., FY 2021). Calendar year is Jan 1 through Dec 31. NIFC burn acres and DSCI use calendar years. Interior and HFR treatment totals use fiscal years. Treatment charts offer a fiscal/calendar toggle; calendar view shifts HFR fiscal data +1 year as an approximate fire-season alignment.
On the data and sources
  • Acres burned: total U.S. figures, all land types, calendar year, NIFC annual statistics. Standardized reporting from 1983; pre-1983 figures are not comparable.
  • 2026 point: partial year through August 27 only (7,971,399 acres). Not a full-year figure.
  • 10-year baseline band: min-to-max of the ten prior calendar years of NIFC acres burned. Computed from chart data, not the CRS flat 7.2M reference.
  • Forest Service treatment: NPR analysis of USFS FACTS database, cross-checked by Center for Western Priorities. Consistent figures from 2023 onward only.
  • Interior Department treatment: BLM, NPS, BIA, and FWS lands, fiscal year starting October 1, doi.gov, 2018-2024.
  • DSCI: annual average of weekly readings, U.S. Drought Monitor API. National (conus) and NWS Western Region (aoi=WR). Available from 2000. 2026 values are 34-week averages through August 25.
  • VPD: western U.S. fire-season mean from gridMET via OPeNDAP (Abatzoglou 2013). May through September, west of 100°W, 1979-2025.
  • ERC: western fire-season mean Energy Release Component from gridMET (NFDRS fuel model G). Same geography and season as VPD. scripts/extend_erc.py; annual averages.
  • KBDI (South): Jan-May mean Keetch-Byram Drought Index for the SE research bbox, computed from gridMET tmmx and precip (scripts/extend_kbdi.py; south-kbdi-annual.csv). Classic Southern Area drought index; exploratory r vs SA acres ≈ 0.20 (n=13), weaker than south VPD.
  • Western acres burned: sum of NICC GACC acres for NW, NR, GB, RM, SW, NO, SO (EB+WB before 2015 Great Basin merge), 2010-2025. Built by scripts/build_western_acres.py from NICC annual reports. Excludes Alaska, Eastern, and Southern GACCs.
  • 2026 forecast: AccuWeather published 2026 wildfire season projection, 5.5 to 8 million acres.
  • Research stations: USDA Forest Service reorganization announced March 31, 2026.
  • Smoke PM2.5: county-mean daily wildfire smoke PM2.5, CONUS, 2006-2023 (ECHO Lab v2.0 beta; scripts/build_smoke_v2_beta_annual.py; smoke-pm25-annual.csv). Childs v1 archive: smoke-pm25-v1-annual.csv. Literature context: Burke et al. 2023 (Nature). Bake-off: smoke-pm25-v2-bakeoff.md.
  • Structures destroyed: NICC SIT/209 national totals 2014-2025 (scripts/extract_nicc_structures.py; structures-destroyed-annual.csv). Pre-2010 gap documented in notes.
  • Federal suppression (Impacts compact + Context): NIFC FS+DOI fiscal costs (suppression-cost-annual.csv).
  • Correlations: Drivers shows strongest co-movement rows first (expand for more pairings and diagnostics). Literature usually ranks ERC ≳ VPD ≫ drought for western large-fire area; page r values are this repository only (correlation-matrix.csv). Not causal evidence.
  • Data processing: drought from U.S. Drought Monitor API; western fire-season VPD from gridMET (Abatzoglou 2013). CSVs and notebooks in the GitHub repository.
